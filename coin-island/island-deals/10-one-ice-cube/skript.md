# 10 – One Ice Cube (2026-11-24)

Format: Island Deals fable · seamless loop · narrator "Titan" (excited) · one-word captions · about 55 s · about 46 shots

THIS SHORT IS A CLEAN TEST OF THE OPENING. Studio shows "viewed vs swiped away" of 84.5 % for Diamonds, but only 69 % for dip v2 and 56 % for gold rock v2. The hypothesis: Diamonds shows one thing per frame on a calm, bright background, the v2 openings are crowded. So here the first two seconds are plain sky and sand behind one subject per shot, with no price tag, no sign, no stall, no aerial and no establishing shot, in the daylight palette of Diamonds. Everything after second 2 follows the normal rules.

Story in three sentences: In the morning heat Squid drags a block of ice across the beach and asks one coin a cube; the Baron finds that outrageous and decides to wait for a discount. The hotter it gets, the smaller the block and the higher the price, and the Baron keeps waiting until one cube is left and it costs everything he owns. He pays, lifts the cube, and it melts to one drop before it reaches his beak, while Squid sits on the gold and sells the meltwater he caught in a bucket.

Why this one: the channel's best short is water for diamonds, and the second best Island Deal was water again. This is the same thirst with a clock in it: the thing he wants shrinks in every shot, so every cut raises the question whether he buys in time. One character, one absurd deal, punished for his own greed (the haggling).

## Characters and what they feel (play these traits in every shot they are in)

- **Baron Goldbelly:** vain, stingy, calls everyone "peasant". He could buy the whole block in the morning and does not, because paying the asking price is beneath him. His arc is the film: smug in the morning, sweating at noon, tongue out and shaking at the end. Shot 36 is his one moment of pure hope (he holds the cube like a jewel), shot 39 is the fall.
- **Squid:** quiet, patient, never argues. He only says the price. His win is not planned revenge, he simply waits. Sigma moment in shot 41.
- **Chad Fisher:** huge, calm, notices nothing. Stands in the sea and fishes while everything boils.
- **Fan crab:** the Baron's servant, fans him with a palm leaf until it faints.

## Direction rules for this short

1. **Opening test (0 to about 2.9 s, the whole first sentence):** behind every subject only plain blue sky and pale sand, nothing else in the frame. One subject per shot. No tag, no sign, no stall, no palm, no yacht, no crabs, no aerial, no wide shot. Clouds only small and far. Each picture is cut on its word and none is longer than about 1 s.
2. **Daylight palette like Diamonds for the whole film:** bright blue sky, pale sand, clean figure colours. NO orange grade. The heat is shown by things, not by colour: heat shimmer over the sand, sweat drops, a white sun disc that is bigger in every act, the drink bubbling, the ice dripping.
3. **The ice is the clock.** In every shot where the block or the cubes appear they are visibly smaller than in the shot before, with a puddle growing under them and a bucket under the cart catching the drip from shot 9 on (it must be visible early so the ending is fair).
4. Price tags appear only AFTER second 3 and only when Squid says a price: "1 COIN", "10", "100", "EVERYTHING". Lettering always whole, never hidden by a hat.
5. All standing rules apply: framing rule, caption safe zone (one band for the opening and the loop end, never on a face, tag or hero object), figures never motion-blurred or hidden before a cut, cut frames rendered with the light of their shot, untouched original character art, no shading pass, faces react on the word, no lip sync, voice not sped up.
6. Loop end: the last frames are frame 0.

## Frame 0 (also the last frame)

The Baron's head and shoulders in the middle of the frame, plain blue sky behind him, a strip of pale sand at the bottom. Shocked face with raised brows and wide eyes, beak open, tongue tip out, three sweat drops. Nothing else in the picture. Caption band below the beak.
